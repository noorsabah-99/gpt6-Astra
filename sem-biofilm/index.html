How a field is read

  1. The instrument data bar is cropped. Pixel size, field width (HFW) and the data-bar height are read from Thermo Fisher/FEI TIFF metadata; other instruments use the scale bar.
  2. Areas you mark (cracks, splitting lines, anatomical recesses, surfaces that are not prepared canal wall) and objects you click (zirconia particles, debris) leave both the numerator and the denominator.
  3. Large charging gradients are evened out, noise is smoothed, and pixels brighter than one fixed grey level, the same for every image, are counted as biofilm. Bright cocci and EPS lie above it; dentine and dark tubule orifices lie below. Specks below the size limit are dropped.
  4. Coverage = biofilm area ÷ evaluated area. It is rounded to a whole percent and placed in the index above, so 33.4% scores 2 and 33.6% scores 3.
  5. Fields within 3 percentage points of a cut-off (5, 33.5, 66.5%) are flagged as borderline. Check them by eye and enter your own score if you disagree; your score is then used everywhere.
  6. Each root third gets one score from its 3 points, and group tables report median, interquartile range and the count of each score.

Automatic coverage depends on constant imaging conditions (accelerating voltage, detector, brightness and contrast). Before relying on it, score a set of fields yourself and check the Examiner agreement tab; a weighted kappa above 0.8 is usually reported as almost perfect agreement.
