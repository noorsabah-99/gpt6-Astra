Reference photos

Photos you have already scored yourself. The app finds the brightness threshold that reproduces your scores on them, then reads every study photo with that threshold. Use 3–5 photos per score level, from different groups and thirds, imaged with the same SEM settings as the study.

Score sheet

Three points per root third. A point takes its score from its photo, or you can type a score yourself; a typed score always wins. The mean of the three points is the score of the third.

How a field is read

  1. The instrument data bar is cropped. Pixel size, field width (HFW) and the data-bar height are read from Thermo Fisher/FEI TIFF metadata; other instruments use the scale bar.
  2. Areas you mark (cracks, splitting lines, anatomical recesses, surfaces that are not prepared canal wall) and objects you click (zirconia particles, debris) leave both the numerator and the denominator.
  3. Large charging gradients are evened out, noise is smoothed, and pixels brighter than one fixed grey level, the same for every image, are counted as biofilm. Bright cocci and EPS lie above it; dentine and dark tubule orifices lie below. Specks below the size limit are dropped.
  4. Coverage = biofilm area ÷ evaluated area. It is rounded to a whole percent and placed in the index above, so 33.4% scores 2 and 33.6% scores 3.
  5. Fields within 3 percentage points of a cut-off (4.5, 33.5, 66.5%) are flagged as borderline. Check them by eye and type your own score if you disagree; your score is then used everywhere.
  6. The threshold comes from your reference photos: the app tries every threshold, keeps those that give each reference the score you gave it, and uses the middle of that range. Leave-one-out agreement tells you how well it should read photos it has not seen.
  7. Each root third scores the mean of its 3 point scores. Group tables report mean ± SD and median with interquartile range of those third scores.

Automatic coverage depends on constant imaging conditions (accelerating voltage, detector, brightness and contrast). Before relying on it, score a set of fields yourself and check the Examiner agreement tab; a weighted kappa above 0.8 is usually reported as almost perfect agreement. Your scores, reference photos and settings are saved with this page; study photos are not stored, only their results, so add a photo again if you want to view or re-score it.
